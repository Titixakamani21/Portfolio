Key Research Insights

What users actually told us

💰

Flexibility Over Digitisation

Users want choice, not just a digital receipt replacing paper.

Users across all segments consistently ranked flexibility as more important than digitisation. They didn't want a digital receipt for its own sake; they wanted options for what to do with the money.
❤️

High Donation Intent, No Path

Many wanted to donate refunds but had zero infrastructure to act on it.

A significant portion of interviewees expressed willingness to donate small refund amounts to charity, but the current system offered no mechanism to do so. The gap between intention and action was entirely structural.
📍

Machine Location Matters

Finding a working, nearby Pfand machine was a real barrier for users.

Users frequently reported travelling to supermarkets only to find machines broken or full. Real-time availability data would significantly reduce wasted trips and frustration.
💡

How this connects back

Research confirmed what I'd observed on the streets but added a crucial layer: the shoppers themselves wanted to help. They weren't indifferent to the people collecting bottles. They simply had no way to act within the existing system.

Make the return experience so seamless that users naturally engage with all their options, including the option to donate.

Insight 01

Eliminate the Paper Layer

Every pain point traces back to the paper receipt. Remove it and the biggest friction disappears.

Insight 02

Choice Creates Engagement

When users can save, donate, or transfer, they return more bottles and engage more deeply.

Insight 03

Small Amounts, Big Impact

Tiny refunds feel meaningless alone. But accumulated in saving pots with interest, they become meaningful.

How might we turn bottle returns from a frustrating chore into a seamless, rewarding experience?

Reflection

What I learned

🔬

Research Drives Everything

Every feature that resonated came from a validated insight. Assumptions rarely matched what users actually needed.

✂️

Fewer Decisions = Better UX

The most impactful iterations removed steps, not added features. The scan flow proved this beautifully.

🔧

Design Systems Pay Off

Investing time upfront made the hi-fi phase faster and kept visuals consistent without constant rework.

What I'd do differently: Test with a larger, more diverse group. Explore retailer partnerships and gamification to keep engagement going long-term.
